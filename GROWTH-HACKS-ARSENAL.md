# Grounded Drops — The Full Growth-Hack Arsenal

83 tactics. Each entry: the mechanism, and the exact Grounded Drops execution. Legality flagged
where it matters (companion legal detail in GROWTH-PLAYBOOK.md §1/§14). This is a menu — the
operating rule is at the bottom: pick 3 engines, execute violently, ignore the rest until one
plateaus.

## A. Product-as-marketing (the packaging IS the channel)

1. **Every wrapper is media you own.** QR on every individual sachet wrapper → "scan to win a
   year of coffee." Every bag handed around a campfire or smoko room becomes a lead-capture
   device. You print labels in-house — this costs nothing.
2. **Golden Ticket drops.** One golden ticket randomly packed into parcels each month (free
   month of coffee / year of coffee). Willy Wonka mechanics are irresistibly shareable — winners
   post it themselves. Keep prize pools under state permit thresholds (NSW $10k/SA $5k/ACT $3k)
   and publish T&Cs.
3. **Glovebox tin.** "Emergency coffee" — a small tin of 4 drip bags designed to live in the ute.
   It's a conversation object; people show it off. Also the perfect $12 add-on/gift/counter SKU.
4. **Numbered drops.** You're literally called *Drops*. Roast-batch releases — "Drop 014: Colombia,
   82 bags, gone when they're gone" — streetwear scarcity mechanics, honestly true (small-batch),
   so fully ACL-safe. Train the list to buy on release day.
5. **Misprint/seconds flash sales.** "Ugly labels, perfect coffee — 30% off, 48 hours." Turns
   waste into an event and gives deal-hunters a legitimate door in without cheapening the brand.
6. **Customer-named blends.** Run naming comps for new blends (name the blend after utes, dogs,
   tracks). Voters = emails; the winner's crew buys it forever because it's *theirs*.
7. **Sticker culture.** 4WD/tradie culture runs on stickers (utes, eskies, toolboxes, swags).
   Print "DEATH BEFORE INSTANT" / Grounded Drops stickers; one in every parcel, handful with every
   sample box. Rolling billboards, ~30c each.
8. **NFC/QR fridge-magnet reorder button** in every subscription welcome box → one-tap reorder
   page. Physical retention hardware for cents.
9. **Handwritten note in every first order.** Highest-converting 60 seconds in DTC: drives
   reviews, photos, and second orders. Scale later with the thermal printer ("handwritten-style"
   is weaker but non-zero).

## B. Viral loops & referral mechanics (upgrade what you built)

10. **Shout a mate a coffee.** Anyone can send a free drip-bag 2-pack to any AU address; you cover
    product, they cover ~$2-3 shipping (or free over trigger). Recipient gets a first-order code.
    It's the Dropbox referral loop with physical coffee — the gift does the selling.
11. **Physical referral cards** in every parcel: "This gets your mate 15% off. When they use it,
    you get a free pack." Unique code pairs; offline referral finally trackable.
12. **Two-sided referral + milestone tiers** in the giveaway app (3 mates = free 3-pack, 10 =
    travel pack, 25 = month of coffee) — persistent codes that survive month rollover (currently
    they die monthly — see playbook §2.1).
13. **Public referral leaderboard** with a monthly prize. You already store per-person referral
    counts; surface them. Competitive tradies will do your marketing to win a hoodie.
14. **Show entrants their odds.** "You hold 7 of 1,240 tickets." Then: "+5 tickets per order" →
    watch entry→purchase conversion. The app already has both numbers.
15. **Everyone-wins consolation code after every draw** (you've run this before — systematise it:
    auto-draft it with the announcement email so it happens every month without you).
16. **Golden referral month.** One month/year: "every referral = double tickets + $5 credit."
    Announced scarcity spikes the loop.

## C. Content & social hacks (documenting beats creating)

17. **Build-in-public, family edition.** Your real story — "I roast coffee in a garage and my
    family depends on this" — is the single most powerful content asset you own. Weekly raw
    updates: revenue milestones, failures, wins, the kids helping pack. This is what goes viral
    and what makes strangers *want* you to win. It costs vulnerability, not money.
18. **The Smoko Rescue series.** Tradies submit photos of their worksite's disgraceful coffee
    setup ("coffee crimes"); you roast (pun intended) the worst ones publicly and send rescue
    boxes. UGC engine + comedy + product demo in one format.
19. **Instant-coffee intervention.** With permission, swap a crew's instant tin for drip bags and
    film reactions. Repeatable, native to TikTok/Reels, infinitely stitchable.
20. **Per-track geo content.** Film the brew at named spots — "Coffee at the top of Billy Goat
    Bluff", "Smoko at Robe Beach camp 27." Location tags + track names = search traffic inside
    TikTok/IG/YouTube that nobody competes for.
21. **Comment-section marketing.** Be the funny/helpful brand reply on every viral camping,
    4WD, and tradie video in AU. Free borrowed reach; brand replies that hit get screenshot and
    reshared.
22. **Duet/stitch bait.** Post honestly spicy takes: "Servo coffee is a $2,000/year scam." Let
    the comments and stitches do distribution. Every claim must be substantiable (ACL) — do the
    math on-screen.
23. **Blind taste tests with real tradies.** Instant vs servo vs drip bag, filmed on sites.
    Predictable, repeatable, shareable format.
24. **Recurring AU moments calendar.** First cold snap of the year ("smoko season opens"),
    Bathurst weekend packs, EOFY "write off your crew's coffee" for ABN holders, Father's Day
    (pitch guides in July), Tradie Appreciation Week. Same 6 posts every year, compounding.
25. **Pinterest + YouTube long-tail.** "Camp kitchen setup" boards and "camp coffee methods
    ranked" videos are evergreen search surfaces with near-zero AU competition; they sell for
    years, not hours.
26. **Podcast sponsorship barter.** Small AU camping/4WD/tradie podcasts need sponsors; offer
    product + affiliate code instead of cash for host-read spots. Costs COGS, sounds like a real
    sponsorship, builds the affiliate stable.

## D. Audience-borrowing & partnerships

27. **Fundraiser army.** The Krispy Kreme model: footy clubs, CFS brigades, school camps sell
    Grounded Drops packs, keep $5-8/unit. Hundreds of motivated sellers, zero CAC, and every
    buyer is a warm future customer with your QR in their pantry. This one scales weirdly well
    in regional SA.
28. **Insert swaps** with 3-5 camping-adjacent brands (jerky, hot sauce, camp gear, fishing
    tackle): your card in their parcels, theirs in yours. Free distribution into perfect-fit
    customer bases, monthly.
29. **Joint giveaways** using YOUR app as the infrastructure (other brands pay Gleam/VYPER for
    what you already built): shared prize pool, both brands email their lists, both lists grow.
    Run one per month with a different partner.
30. **Newsletter swaps** with AU camping/4WD newsletters and creators (each mails their own
    consented list — fully Spam Act clean).
31. **Van fitout & camper-trailer builders**: a starter pack in every build handover ("first brew
    on us in your new rig"). Buyers of $80k rigs photograph everything in them.
32. **Caravan parks & campground kiosks**: counter caddies + guest welcome sachets with QR.
    The park literally hands your sampling to fresh campers daily.
33. **Tool/workwear brand tie-ins**: "free smoko pack with every [boot/tool] purchase" — they buy
    wholesale from you as a promo cost; you get sampling + their audience.
34. **RTOs and licensing schools** (white card courses, truck licences): a drip bag + code card
    in every course pack. Every new tradie in SA meets you on day one.
35. **Gym/CrossFit box partnerships**: pre-workout black coffee culture; wholesale + member code.
36. **Cleaning/turnover companies for Airbnbs**: they restock consumables across dozens of
    properties — one deal = distribution to hundreds of guest stays (playbook §5c).

## E. Guerrilla & IRL (dawn raids)

37. **The 6am job-site coffee run.** Once a week, rock up to a big site/depot at smoko with a
    thermos urn and 100 drip bags. 100% ICP density, $50 of product, and film it (tactic #19/#23
    content). Ask the site manager first — that conversation is itself a B2B lead.
38. **Bunnings-adjacent sampling**: partner with the club running the sausage sizzle — "coffee
    donated by Grounded Drops" sign + sachet handouts. Charity goodwill + tradie foot traffic.
39. **Boat ramps & trailheads at dawn** on Saturdays: hand sachets to people launching. They're
    about to be cold, tired, and coffee-less — perfect-moment sampling.
40. **Honesty boxes** at caravan parks/general stores in camping corridors: a caddy, a price, a
    PayID QR. Regional AU honesty-box culture is real and it's free retail.
41. **Windscreen sachets at 4WD meets/shows car parks** (sachet + card under wiper). Check venue
    rules; handing to humans always beats wipers anyway.
42. **Community noticeboards** (IGA, post offices, caravan parks) along tourist corridors:
    "Local SA roaster — free sample pack, scan here."
43. **Roastery open day / pack-your-own-box events.** Scarcity ("20 spots"), charge cost,
    everyone posts it, everyone subscribes.
44. **Sponsor a local footy club's canteen coffee** — signage + announcer mentions + the whole
    town tastes it every Saturday.

## F. PR & earned-media hacks

45. **The free-coffee campaign IS a news story.** "SA garage roaster opts out of the ad market,
    posts free coffee to hard-working crews instead" — pitch it to SmartCompany, AdNews, ABC
    Adelaide, The Advertiser small-biz desk. Your Tier-3 outreach doubles as a PR stunt. This is
    the highest-leverage framing of the idea you already had.
46. **The State of Smoko Report.** Survey 500 tradies (fun questions: instant vs real, smoko
    length, worst site coffee story) via your list + partner audiences. Publish annually. Media
    can't resist original data with a funny name; every article links to you (SEO flywheel), and
    it makes you *the* authority on site coffee. Costs a prize draw.
47. **SourceBottle daily** (free AU journalist call-outs): answer every small-biz, food, camping,
    and family-business call-out. 10 minutes/day; compounding media bank.
48. **Award-entry season**: SA local business awards, delicious. Produce Awards, telco/bank
    small-biz awards. Winning = "award-winning" on every label forever; entering = free
    deadline-driven PR.
49. **Stunt records.** "Most pour-over coffees brewed at a single campsite" with a 4WD club —
    silly, photogenic, local-news catnip.
50. **Newsjack fuel/cost-of-living cycles**: "the $6 servo coffee vs 90c drip bag" math, offered
    to journos every time coffee prices make news.
51. **Cameo-style shoutouts from AU tradie/4WD identities** — many do paid personal videos for
    $50-150; use as content + social proof (disclose it's paid if used as advertising).

## G. Deals platforms & marketplaces (AU-specific)

52. **OzBargain launch.** A genuinely strong deal (40% first box) posted per their self-promo
    rules (declared rep account). A front-page OzBargain deal moves hundreds of orders overnight
    and OzBargainers write brutal-but-gold feedback. Deal hunters churn — capture them into the
    giveaway/subscription funnel immediately.
53. **r/adelaide, r/australia small-biz threads, r/AussieFrugal** — per-sub self-promo rules,
    founder-voice launch posts ("I roast coffee in my garage in Adelaide, AMA") outperform ads
    and are welcomed where stealth marketing is banned.
54. **Facebook Marketplace & Gumtree listings** for sample boxes — free classifieds traffic;
    unconventional for consumables, which is why nobody's there. Local pickup doubles as
    meet-the-roaster.
55. **Amazon AU** for buy-ready searches (fees/economics in playbook §11); inserts in marketplace
    parcels limited to the giveaway QR (no "buy direct instead" — that breaches marketplace ToS).
56. **eBay/Catch gift bundles** at Christmas/Father's Day only — seasonal shelf space, no
    year-round margin drag.
57. **TikTok Shop: parked** (not in AU as of mid-2026). Pre-register interest; first-mover
    advantage if it lands.

## H. SEO/AEO & data hacks

58. **Programmatic long-tail pages**: "coffee setup for the Simpson Desert crossing", "best
    coffee for the Heysen Trail", per-track/per-campsite pages. Zero competition, exact-intent
    traffic, and AI assistants cite whoever answers the question directly.
59. **Own the comparison**: honest "drip bags vs instant vs pods vs plunger" data pages (taste,
    cost/cup, weight, waste). Google's demotion of affiliate roundups left this space open; being
    the truthful comparison source is legal (ACL: every claim substantiated) and AI-quotable.
60. **Reddit answer-farming for AEO**: genuinely useful answers in every "coffee while camping"
    thread — those threads rank on Google AND get ingested by AI assistants as recommendations.
    Answer as the founder, disclosed. The 2026 version of SEO.
61. **Free calculator tool**: "What's your servo coffee habit costing you?" — shareable, linkable,
    tradie-bait, captures email for the full report.
62. **FAQ/product schema everywhere** so AI Overviews quote you; write the literal question as
    the H2 and answer it in the first sentence.
63. **Google Business Profile** (service-area roaster): weekly posts, review surface, "coffee
    roaster Adelaide" local-pack presence. Free and almost nobody in the niche does it well.

## I. Offer & pricing hacks

64. **Founders Club — first 100 subscribers**: locked price forever, name on the roastery wall,
    first dibs on drops. Status + scarcity + upfront recurring revenue NOW.
65. **Prepaid annual sub at a discount** = working capital today (this is your cheapest possible
    financing — better than any loan).
66. **Free + shipping tripwire**: sample 2-pack, customer pays ~$5 postage. You buy a *paying
    customer with card on file* for a couple of dollars, then the flows (playbook §4) do the rest.
67. **Decoy bundle pricing**: a $99 "Full Setup" makes the $45 bundle the obvious buy (and some
    take the $99).
68. **Gift-with-purchase thresholds**: enamel mug at $80 spend — AOV lever + the mug is branded
    media at every campsite it visits.
69. **Pay-what-you-want week** (floor = shipping) for sample boxes, once, loudly: list-builder +
    press hook + "fair go" brand equity.
70. **ABN/EOFY angle**: "Crew coffee is a business expense — sort your smoko before June 30."
    B2B flash window with genuine deadline (the tax year — can't get more ACL-safe).
71. **Back-in-stock & roast-day SMS/email alerts** (consented): manufactured urgency that's
    completely true — the roast really does sell out.
72. **Mystery "roaster's cut" box** monthly: odd lots, experiments, overruns at a sharp price —
    moves inventory, feels like insider access.

## J. Retention & LTV hacks

73. **Consumption-timed reorder nudges**: a 10-pack ≈ 2 weeks for a daily drinker; email/SMS lands
    day 11 with one-tap reorder. Consumables + known burn rate = retention on rails.
74. **Skip/pause/swap before cancel** in the sub flow (halves churn vs cancel-only), plus a
    "moving house? going remote for work?" pause reason that starts a conversation.
75. **Surprise-and-delight batch**: every Nth subscription box gets a free experimental bag +
    "tell us what you reckon" — reviews, retention, R&D in one.
76. **Win-back with the story, not a discount**: "The roastery's still here because of customers
    like you — here's what's new since March." Then the code.

## K. Grey-but-legal (edges marked)

77. **Coupon-site seeding**: publish your own controlled codes (min-spend floors) on Honey/
    cashback/coupon sites so checkout code-hunters convert instead of bouncing.
78. **Competitor-comparison pages** ("Grounded Drops vs [big instant brand]") — legal if every
    line is true and provable; keep the receipts. Aggressive and almost never done properly.
79. **Competitor stockist-list prospecting** — their store-locator pages are public; contact by
    phone/in-person/post/LinkedIn (not bulk email — Spam Act).
80. **Direct mail offensive**: the Spam Act doesn't touch post. Postcard campaigns to depots,
    smoko rooms, caravan parks. Empty letterboxes, full inboxes — go where the attention is.
81. **One-to-one B2B email under "conspicuous publication"** (published business address,
    role-relevant, personally written, records kept, no automation) — the only legal cold-email
    lane in Australia; stay strictly inside it.
82. **Buying established AU camping Facebook groups/pages**: possible and not illegal, but it
    breaches Facebook ToS (groups can be removed, accounts banned) and misleading-admin conduct
    can raise ACL issues. Higher-integrity version: *partner with* admins (monthly member
    giveaway, mod-approved) — nearly all the reach, none of the risk.
83. **Waitlists & velvet ropes**: "Drop 015 waitlist — 82 bags, waitlist gets 24h early access."
    Legal because the scarcity is real. Waitlist = email capture = the whole flywheel again.

## The operating rule (this is the actual hack)

Every successful zero-budget brand runs 2-3 of these as ENGINES (daily/weekly, measured,
non-negotiable) and treats the rest as a menu for later. Spreading across 20 = doing none.

**My pick for your specific hand (assets: giveaway app, label printer, ForestrySA, a true family
story, drip bags = perfect sample unit):**

- **Engine 1 — B2B free-box machine** (B2B-PROSPECTS.md): 5 qualified boxes + follow-ups/week.
  Pays the bills with recurring accounts. Amplify once with the PR framing (#45).
- **Engine 2 — The wrapper flywheel**: QR on every sachet → giveaway → fixed referral loop
  (#1, #10-#16). Every unit shipped — retail, B2B, sampling — recruits the next customer.
- **Engine 3 — Build-in-public founder content** (#17) 3x/week, cut with smoko-rescue/taste-test
  formats (#18, #23). The story is the moat; nobody can copy it.
- **Quarterly bolt-ons**: one OzBargain moment (#52), one State of Smoko report (#46), one
  fundraiser-army pilot (#27), Father's Day pitch wave (July — now).
