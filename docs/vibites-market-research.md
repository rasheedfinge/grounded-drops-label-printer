## 0. Scoping assumption (please confirm)

"Vibites" isn't a public brand — no trading presence, no ARTG entry, nothing on the Grounded Drops
Shopify catalogue (which is currently all coffee, mugs and subscriptions). So this brief treats it as a
**new Grounded Drops venture in the functional-supplement space**, prompted by the Body Armour sleep ad.

The name implies a **chewable / bite format**, but the reference ad is a **powder**. Those are materially
different businesses (see §6 — the dosing constraint is the single most important finding in this brief).
Everything below covers the category and both formats; once you confirm the intended format I can go a
level deeper on COGS, manufacturers and formulation.

## 2. Why every Australian sleep product looks the same

This is the structural fact that shapes the whole category, and it's the one most new entrants miss.

**Melatonin is not available as a supplement in Australia.** It's a scheduled medicine — Schedule 3
(pharmacist-only) for modified-release ≤2 mg for adults 55+, or ≤5 mg immediate-release for jet lag in
adults 18+ in packs of ≤10; **everything else is Schedule 4, prescription-only**. It cannot go in a
complementary medicine.

Consequences:

1. AU sleep products are forced onto the same shortlist of permitted ingredients — magnesium, glycine,
   GABA, ashwagandha, passionflower, ziziphus, valerian, lemon balm, hops, California poppy. Body Armour,
   Switch Nutrition, Primabolics, Red Dragon and Blackmores are drawing from one deck of cards.
2. **Formulation is therefore not a defensible differentiator.** Anyone can clone the Body Armour label in
   a week. Differentiation has to come from format, brand, ritual, taste, or channel.
3. It also means AU consumers who want melatonin buy it overseas — a persistent grey-market leak the TGA
   has publicly warned about, and a demand pocket that legitimate AU brands convert by offering a
   "melatonin-free, actually legal here" alternative.

**Regulatory cost of entry (Listed medicine / AUST L):**

- An **Australian-resident sponsor** is legally responsible for compliance, labelling, advertising and safety.
- Every active and excipient must appear on the TGA Permitted Ingredients Determination.
- Manufacture must be under TGA GMP (overseas sites need GMP clearance, and you must audit and document them).
- Indicative costs: **~$900–$1,200 ARTG application fee per product**, plus **~$5,000–$20,000** regulatory
  consultant fees depending on complexity.
- Advertising is restricted to **permitted indications** — you can say "supports restful sleep", you cannot
  say "treats insomnia". This constrains ad creative in ways coffee marketing does not.

## 4. Demand: is the category actually growing?

Signals are positive, but treat the headline market-size numbers with suspicion — they come from
report-vendor sites with wide variance and inconsistent definitions.

**Solid:**
- **"Sleepmaxxing"** is a genuine, sustained consumer behaviour — stacking supplements, wearables, mouth
  tape, "sleepy girl mocktails" (tart cherry + magnesium), elaborate wind-down routines. It has made
  magnesium powder a mainstream cultural object rather than a supplement-shop item.
- Gen Z and millennials are disproportionately increasing wellness spend; ~30% say they're prioritising
  wellness "a lot more" than a year ago.
- In Australia, magnesium **glycinate and L-threonate** now command an estimated 40–55% of clean-label
  retail value, at **60–120% price premiums over magnesium oxide** — consumers are demonstrably paying up
  for the better-absorbed forms.
- **Powders are the fastest-growing format** in magnesium supplements (~9% CAGR through 2034).

**Treat cautiously:**
- "Australia functional gummies market USD 3.06 bn (2024) → USD 8.3 bn (2033), 11.8% CAGR" (IMARC). The
  direction is right; the absolute number looks inflated for a market of 27 m people. Use it as a
  trend signal, not a plan input.

**Counter-signal worth taking seriously:** 2026 forecasting is now flagging **consumer fatigue with
sleep-product stacking** — buyers becoming more selective and questioning whether the next purchase
actually improves sleep. A late entrant with an undifferentiated stack walks into that headwind.

## 6. The format question (the critical finding)

If Vibites is intended as a **bite / chewable / gummy**, there is a hard physical constraint:

Body Armour's serve is **~4.5 g of actives in an 8 g scoop**, of which 3 g is glycine alone. A standard
gummy is 3–5 g *total* and is mostly sugar/pectin carrier — realistically 300–800 mg of actives.
**You cannot put a clinically-shaped sleep dose into a gummy.** Every sleep gummy on the market solves this
by leaning on melatonin (potent at 0.5–3 mg) — which, per §2, **is not legal in an Australian supplement**.

So the honest options are:

| Format | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Gummy / bite, AU market** | Structurally weak. No melatonin to lean on, no room for glycine/GABA doses. You'd be selling a low-dose herbal chewable at a premium — vulnerable to "does nothing" reviews. Also: sugar-based confection sits badly against a clean, grounded brand. |
| **Powder tub, 30 serves** | Proven, but you'd be entrant #10 at $2/serve against Switch at $0.83–1.67, with no formulation edge. |
| **Single-serve sachets** ⭐ | The one genuinely open position. Nobody in the AU sleep category owns portable single-serve — and it is *exactly* the Grounded Drops competence: you already sell single-serve drip coffee bags to travellers. Same manufacturing logic, same customer, same shelf in the same bag. Premium per-serve pricing is defensible on convenience ($2.50–3.00/serve is credible in sachet format where it isn't in a tub). |
| **Coffee-adjacent daytime functional** | Adjacent play — functional coffee is ~$4.5 bn globally and projected to roughly double by 2035, with sachets the growth format. Lower regulatory burden than a sleep listing if positioned as food. |

## 7. Where the actual opportunity is for Grounded Drops

**The strategic story writes itself: you already own the morning ritual. Sleep is the other end of the
same day.** "Grounded" as a brand word works better at 9pm than at 7am — that's a gift, not a stretch.

Four differentiation levers available, ranked by defensibility:

1. **Format — travel/single-serve.** Your existing DNA. Nobody in AU sleep owns it. Highest-conviction lever.
2. **Native Australian botanicals.** Roogenic has proven Jilungin can carry a brand. Lemon myrtle, Jilungin,
   native flavour systems give you a story no Brisbane sports-nutrition brand can copy, and it aligns with
   an outdoors/Australian coffee brand far better than a black tub with a lightning bolt on it.
3. **Ritual pairing / bundle.** Morning drip bag + evening wind-down sachet as one subscription. This is a
   retention play more than an acquisition play, and it's where your economics beat everyone's.
4. **Taste.** Genuinely underrated — the category is a graveyard of chalky, over-acidified powders, and you
   are a business that already sells on flavour craft.

**Recommended shape:** a melatonin-free, native-botanical **single-serve evening sachet**, priced
$2.50–3.00/serve in packs of 10 and 30, sold subscription-default, launched to the existing coffee list
first with a paid sample/trial pack (mirroring Body Armour's sample SKU), and only opened to cold Meta
traffic once the list-launch validates repeat rate.

**Do not** launch a gummy into the AU sleep category. The regulatory maths doesn't work.

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory burden (ARTG, sponsor, GMP, permitted indications) | High | Real cost and real timeline; also constrains ad copy in ways coffee doesn't. Budget $10–25k and several months before first sale. |
| Category crowding + 2026 "sleep-stack fatigue" | High | You're entering late. Differentiation must be format/brand, not formula. |
| Formula is not defensible | Medium | Accept it. Compete on brand, format and channel. |
| iHerb / Chemist Warehouse price anchoring | Medium | 40–70% cheaper on single ingredients. Never compete on ingredient cost-per-mg. |
| Brand stretch from coffee to supplements | Medium | Mitigated by the morning/evening ritual narrative; aggravated if the product looks like sports nutrition. |
| Gummy dosing constraint | High (if gummy) | See §6. This one is disqualifying, not manageable. |
| MOQ / working capital | Medium | Contract manufacturers are increasingly serving low-MOQ DTC runs, but a listed medicine run is a bigger cheque than a coffee run. |

## 9. Suggested next steps

1. **Confirm what Vibites is** — bite, powder, sachet, or coffee-adjacent. Everything downstream forks here.
2. If sleep: get quotes from 2–3 **TGA-GMP AU contract manufacturers** for a sachet-format listed medicine,
   with MOQs and per-unit costs at 5k/10k/25k units.
3. **Validate on your own list before spending on Meta** — a waitlist or pre-order to the existing coffee
   base will tell you more, faster and cheaper, than any ad test.
4. Pull the Body Armour ad library (Meta Ad Library, `bodyarmour.com.au`) to see which sleep creatives
   they're scaling and how long each has been live — running time is the best free signal of what's working.
5. Decide on the native-botanical angle early — it drives formulation, supply chain and brand simultaneously.
